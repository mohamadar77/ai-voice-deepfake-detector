Slide 2 — Motivation

Why this matters

  • Voice cloning is cheap and fast: a few seconds of audio, a public model, a convincing fake. Real incidents include phone scams and deepfake political audio.
  • Automatic detection is a measurable signal, not a verdict — it helps human reviewers focus, not replace them.
  • This project: build a detector, measure it honestly, show its limits.
The detector outputs a score, not ground truth. Every response carries a limitations notice.

Emphasize that the detector is a score, not a claim of fact.

Slide 4 — Evaluation Integrity

Keeping the test honest

  • Strict partition walls: no evaluation data in training, tuning, threshold setting, or checkpoint selection.
  • Split before transformation: windows and augmentations assigned to their original clip and partition.
  • Speaker-grouped reservation: 5 of 20 development speakers reserved for the operating threshold (SHA-256 ranked).
  • Protocol frozen before inspection: candidates, thresholds, conditions and metrics locked before any evaluation prediction was read.
  • One score per clip: mean of 4-second window logits, aggregated by clip_id.
Honest evaluation is the contribution, not the model.

This is the boring-but-crucial part. Spend time on why it matters, not on the mechanics.

Slide 5 — LR Baseline

MFCC + Logistic Regression

Audio → Windows → 40-bin MFCC → Pool (mean, var, skew) → LR
  • Real audio only — no zero-pad contamination ("option D").
  • 3 hyperparameter settings, class-balanced weights.
  • Selected on development EER; threshold at 5% false-positive rate on 5 reserved speakers.
  • Purpose: a reference that the CNN must beat on the same protocol.

Explain that the baseline exists to give the CNN comparison meaning, not to be competitive.

Augmentation helps dramatically: augmented CNNs hold up under 10 dB noise (EER ~20%) while the clean CNN drops to 61.4% (below chance).
Thresholds don't transfer: clean-dev thresholds flag 25–33% of human clips under noise/codec. Negative result published, not hidden.

The codec_24k and noise conditions overlap with training augmentation — these are matched-condition results, disclosed.

Slide 9 — Per-Attack & Failures

Not all attacks are equal

  • A09 nearly solved by every CNN (EER ≈ 0%).
  • A17 is hardest for all models (CNN EER 17–22%).
  • A10, A13, A15 remain challenging.
  • Known-algorithm attacks A16/A19 (reusing A04/A06) are much easier (CNN EER 0.4–6.5%).

Silence shortcut diagnostic

  • Silence trimming raises CNN EER from 7–12% to 20–23%.
  • Evidence of partial reliance on silence cues — a shortcut diagnostic, not proof.
Honest reporting: some attacks are hard, one model seed has a below-chance result, and there is evidence of a silence shortcut.

Spend time on what we learned from failures, not on listing every attack. The shortcut finding is the most interesting negative result.

Slide 12 — Conclusions

What we learned

  1. A 23.7K-parameter CNN on log-Mel spectrograms cuts the LR baseline's EER roughly in half on unseen attacks (6.7–11.8% vs 19.9%).
  2. Training augmentation (noise + codec) dramatically improves robustness at some cost to clean accuracy.
  3. Seed variance is large — single-seed results can be misleading.
  4. Thresholds set on clean audio do not transfer under distortion.
  5. There is evidence of partial reliance on silence cues.
  6. Honest evaluation matters more than the number: frozen protocol, published negatives, disclosed limitations.

Future work (Phase 2)

  • ASVspoof 2021 DF transfer evaluation
  • Frozen wav2vec 2.0 embeddings (if compute allows)
  • Calibration and abstention on uncertain scores
Not claimed: this is not an official leaderboard result, not a speaker verifier, and not proof of authenticity.

End on the methodology message: the honest evaluation is the contribution.
